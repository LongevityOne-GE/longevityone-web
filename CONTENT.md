#### Content (English):

There is a number most people know by heart: the year they were born. From that number, the world draws its conclusions — about what medications you should take, what risks you carry, how many productive years lie ahead. Modern science, however, has a problem with this approach. The year of your birth is one of the least informative numbers in your biology.

**Two Ways of Counting**

Your chronological age counts time — and nothing more. It measures how many rotations the Earth has made around the sun since you arrived in the world, indifferent to whether those years were spent in peak health or chronic stress, sleeping eight hours a night or four, moving daily or barely at all.

Biological age is something fundamentally different. It measures how your cells, tissues, and organs are actually functioning right now. Two people born in the same year can have biological ages that differ by a decade or more — and that gap has real consequences for how long and how well they live.

This is not a hypothesis. A landmark longitudinal study published in *Nature* that tracked nearly a thousand participants born in the same year found that biological ageing rates varied enormously between individuals. By the age of 38, some had the biological profile of a 28-year-old while others resembled a 61-year-old. Same chronological age. Completely different biological realities.

**How Is Biological Age Measured?**

For decades, scientists searched for a reliable molecular clock inside the human body. They examined telomere length, hormone levels, and dozens of other markers — each with significant limitations. Then, in 2013, biostatistician Steve Horvath published what became known as the "Horvath clock": a method of estimating biological age through DNA methylation patterns.

DNA methylation is an epigenetic process — a mechanism by which your lifestyle, environment, and habits effectively annotate your DNA, switching certain genes on or off. These patterns shift in predictable, measurable ways as we age. By analysing specific sites across the genome, scientists can now estimate biological age with remarkable accuracy. Subsequent generations of epigenetic clocks have refined this further, adding not just a static biological age measurement but a dynamic pace-of-ageing score — measuring how fast you are ageing right now, not just how far you have aged so far.

This is what TrueDiagnostic's TrueAge test does. It analyses hundreds of thousands of methylation sites to calculate both your biological age and your current pace of ageing. Two people with the same biological age can be ageing at very different speeds — and that rate is what determines where they will be in ten years.

**Why the Gap Matters**

People whose biological age is lower than their chronological age tend to have stronger cardiovascular health, more robust immune function, lower rates of metabolic disease, and significantly reduced risk of premature mortality. Studies of people who reach exceptional old age consistently find one shared characteristic: their biological age is substantially younger than their calendar age — in some cohorts, by fifteen to twenty years or more.

The factors driving this gap are well understood. Chronic sleep deprivation, sustained psychological stress, poor nutrition, physical inactivity, and excess visceral fat all accelerate biological ageing. Conversely, regular aerobic exercise, restorative sleep, whole-food nutrition, effective stress management, and strong social relationships are consistently associated with slower epigenetic ageing.

This is the central insight: biological age is not fixed. Unlike the year you were born, it can change — in either direction — based on the choices you make and the targeted interventions you pursue.

**The Value of Knowing Your Number**

Discovering that your biological age is younger than your chronological age is genuinely encouraging — confirmation that how you are living is working. Discovering the opposite is not cause for alarm. It is cause for action.

At Longevity One, TrueAge testing is the foundation of our Longevity programme. It transforms the conversation from a vague "you should take care of your health" to a precise "your cells are functioning like someone ten years older — and here is the exact plan to change that." Twelve weeks later, we retest. The before-and-after comparison is not a theory. It is measurable proof.

Your passport age is fixed. Your biological age does not have to be.

#### Content (English):

Every January, millions of people start a new diet. They count calories, cut carbohydrates, or eliminate entire food groups with genuine determination. By March, most have stopped. By the following January, many are trying again with a different plan — and arriving at the same outcome.

This is not a failure of willpower. It is a failure of method. Traditional diets are built on a fundamental assumption that research repeatedly challenges: that human metabolism is essentially uniform, and a calorie deficit works the same way for everyone.

It does not.

**Your Metabolism Is Unique**

Your Resting Metabolic Rate — the number of calories your body burns simply to maintain basic functions while at rest — accounts for 60 to 75 percent of your total daily energy expenditure. This number varies dramatically between individuals. Two people of identical height, weight, and age can have resting metabolic rates that differ by 300 to 500 calories per day. That is the equivalent of a full meal. A diet plan designed for one of them will leave the other either chronically underfed or insufficiently challenged.

The standard equations used to estimate resting metabolic rate — which underpin virtually every generic nutrition plan and calorie-counting application — routinely diverge from individual reality by a meaningful margin. Research using indirect calorimetry, the clinical gold standard, consistently finds that population-average formulas overestimate or underestimate an individual's actual rate significantly. The only accurate way to determine your resting metabolic rate is to measure it directly. This is what PNOE does.

By analysing exhaled breath in real time, PNOE determines with clinical precision exactly how many calories your body burns at rest, what ratio of carbohydrates to fat you use for fuel, and how your metabolism responds to physical effort. The result is a personalised metabolic profile — not an estimate derived from population averages, but your actual biology.

**The Adaptation Problem**

There is a second reason traditional diets fail, and it is biological rather than motivational: metabolic adaptation. When caloric intake is restricted significantly, the body interprets this as a threat to survival and responds by reducing its resting metabolic rate. Research has demonstrated that aggressive caloric restriction can suppress resting metabolic rate by up to 20 percent. For a person burning 1,500 calories at rest, this means burning 300 fewer calories per day — even while faithfully adhering to the diet.

This is the mechanism behind the yo-yo effect. Weight is lost, the metabolism slows, progress stalls, motivation collapses. The diet ends. Weight returns — sometimes exceeding what was originally lost — because the body, now operating with a suppressed metabolism, stores fat more efficiently as a survival response. Long-term follow-up studies of people who lost significant weight through aggressive dieting have found that metabolic suppression can persist for years after the diet has ended.

**The Role of Your Gut**

There is a third dimension that virtually no conventional diet addresses: the gut microbiome. The trillions of microorganisms living in your intestines play a central role in how you extract energy from food, regulate blood sugar, manage systemic inflammation, and modulate hunger hormones. This is not peripheral biology — it is foundational to how your body processes everything you eat.

Research has demonstrated that two people eating identical meals can have dramatically different blood sugar and metabolic responses, driven primarily by differences in gut microbiome composition. The same food that behaves as a slow-release energy source in one person can provoke a significant blood sugar spike in another. This means that the same nominally healthy diet can produce entirely different outcomes depending on whose gut is processing it.

Enbiosis microbiome analysis identifies the specific composition of your gut flora through genetic sequencing of the microbial DNA present in a stool sample. The result is not generic dietary advice but a personalised nutritional map aligned with the actual biology of your intestines — including specific foods and nutrients that support your microbiome, and those that disrupt it.

**What Works Instead**

The solution is not a better diet. It is a better method. Measure first, prescribe second.

Every metabolic programme at Longevity One begins with PNOE testing to establish your exact resting metabolic rate and fat-burning profile. For clients seeking deeper insight, Enbiosis microbiome analysis is added to understand how your gut processes what you eat. From these two precise data points, a nutritional approach is designed that works with your specific biology — not against it.

There is no willpower required when the plan actually fits. The yo-yo effect disappears when the prescription is accurate. Weight management becomes the predictable outcome of a scientific process — not a test of character.

#### Content (English):

Your annual check-up almost certainly includes cholesterol, blood pressure, blood glucose, and possibly an ECG. It almost certainly does not include VO₂ Max. This is a significant omission — because of all the numbers medicine can measure about a living human body, VO₂ Max may be the single most powerful predictor of how long that person will live.

**What VO₂ Max Is**

VO₂ Max is the maximum rate at which your body can consume oxygen during intense physical effort, expressed in millilitres of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute. It is the gold standard measure of cardiorespiratory fitness — the combined efficiency of your heart, lungs, blood vessels, and muscles working together under demand.

As a single marker, VO₂ Max reflects the health of nearly every major system in your body simultaneously. A high VO₂ Max means your heart is pumping effectively, your lungs are exchanging gases efficiently, your blood vessels are delivering oxygen without resistance, and your mitochondria are converting that oxygen into energy powerfully. It is, in the most literal sense, a measure of your body's capacity for life.

**What the Research Shows**

The evidence connecting VO₂ Max to longevity is among the strongest in all of medicine. A large-scale study published in JAMA Network Open tracked over 120,000 adults through standardised exercise testing. The finding was unambiguous: individuals with the lowest VO₂ Max had a fourfold higher mortality risk than those in the highest category. No other measured variable — including smoking status, hypertension, diabetes, or obesity — showed a stronger statistical association with survival.

Multiple subsequent studies have reinforced this with striking consistency. Research published in a leading cardiology journal found that each meaningful unit increase in VO₂ Max is associated with a 13 to 15 percent reduction in all-cause mortality risk. A long-term Norwegian study following participants across four decades found that each unit improvement in VO₂ Max was associated with approximately 45 additional days of life expectancy.

Longevity medicine physicians who have written extensively on this topic note that moving from the lowest fitness quartile to merely below average — a modest but achievable improvement for most people — is associated with roughly a 50 percent reduction in all-cause mortality risk. Moving from the lowest quartile to above average is associated with approximately 70 percent. No pharmaceutical intervention tested to date approaches these figures for mortality reduction.

**The Decline You Need to Know About**

After the age of 30, VO₂ Max declines at approximately 1 percent per year in sedentary individuals — accelerating to closer to 2 percent after 50. By the age of 60, an inactive person may have lost 30 percent or more of their peak cardiorespiratory capacity. This represents a substantial reduction not just in athletic ability but in the body's fundamental resilience.

This decline is not inevitable. With the right training approach, it can be meaningfully slowed, halted, or partially reversed at virtually any age. But it must be actively managed. The challenge is that most people have no idea where they stand. Standard medical check-ups do not measure VO₂ Max. Consumer wearable estimates carry wide error margins. The only clinically accurate method is direct metabolic testing — the kind PNOE provides.

**How to Improve It**

Improving VO₂ Max requires a specific and evidence-based combination of training approaches. Zone 2 aerobic training — sustained effort at moderate intensity, typically at a pace where conversation remains possible — builds the mitochondrial density and cardiovascular infrastructure that underlies a high VO₂ Max. Two to three sessions per week at 45–60 minutes each forms the foundation.

Supplemented by one weekly high-intensity session specifically targeting the upper limit of aerobic capacity — intervals performed at or near VO₂ Max effort — meaningful improvement becomes achievable within eight to twelve weeks. The combination is well supported by research and practically accessible to anyone who is not managing a cardiac contraindication.
